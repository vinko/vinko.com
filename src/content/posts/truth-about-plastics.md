Today the differences in plastic types, we encounter every day, were brought to my attention.

![Plastic #1](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/31/Plastic-recyc-01.svg) ![Plastic #2](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/18/Plastic-recyc-02.svg) ![Plastic #3](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/30/Plastic-recyc-03.svg) ![Plastic #4](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5a/Plastic-recyc-04.svg) ![Plastic #5](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0c/Plastic-recyc-05.svg) ![Plastic #6](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d7/Plastic-recyc-06.svg) ![Plastic #7](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6d/Plastic-recyc-07.svg)  
  
  
Have you seen these symbols above? More details can be found on the Wikipedia page on [plastics](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic#The_environment "Wikipeida definition for plastics").

The above are the symbols devised by the [Society of the Plastics Industry](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_the_Plastics_Industry "Society of the Plastics Industry") to mark plastic items we use for recycling purpose. As a consumer you can tell from the symbols whether a piece of plastic is recyclable or not.

So next time before you place that piece of plastic in the recycling bin you should check for these symbols.
